Packs Home screen Interactions Recommendation

Three pack directions, three home‑screen directions.

Nothing in the app has changed — this is a gallery so you can pick a direction. Everything uses the real palette (Theme.swift), real set names and real prices ($10 / $30 / $75 / $160 / $400, box = 11×). Packs and phone screens are live HTML: hover a pack for the foil sheen, click one to tear it open, tap around the phone screens.

Goal 1 · make a pack feel physical Goal 2 · one obvious action per screen Goal 3 · show progress without a wall of text

1 · The pack

Today a pack is a rounded rectangle with a gradient and an emoji. None of the cues that make a real pack feel like a pack are there: the crimped heat‑seal edge, the mylar sheen, the bulge of cards inside, the foil‑stamped set art. Each option below fixes that a different way.

Option P1

Foil Wrapper

A real mylar booster wrapper: serrated crimp top and bottom, holographic sheen, wrinkle highlights, a bulge where the cards sit, and the set’s sigil foil‑stamped dead centre like actual packaging.

Why this one

  • Reads as a pack instantly. The crimp and the sheen are the two cues your eye uses to identify a foil wrapper.
  • The art is the hero. The set sigil sits centred and large on a clean wrapper — no fine print competing with it, so the pack reads at a glance and at any size.
  • Scales down cleanly to a 64pt shop thumbnail — the silhouette still reads as a pack.
  • Set identity comes from the element palette plus a foil‑stamped set name, so all five sets share one wrapper template and never look samey.

Cost

One new PackWrapper SwiftUI view: a zig‑zag Shape for the crimp, a TimelineView sheen (already used in SealedPackView), and 3–4 gradient overlays. No assets.

Option P2

Sealed Bundle

The pack is the cards: six branded card backs stacked and slightly fanned, wrapped in a paper belly band with a wax‑style seal. You can see exactly what you bought.

Why this one

  • “6 cards” is shown, not stated. The stack depth is the count.
  • Best transition into the reveal — the bundle fans into the six reveal slots, so the pack literally becomes the cards. No cut, no dissolve.
  • Reuses the CardBack view you already ship, so it’s the cheapest option to build.
  • Boxes read as a taller stack of bundles.

Trade‑off

Less “sealed product” tension than a wrapper — you’re looking at cards, not at a mystery. Slightly weaker as a tiny shop thumbnail, since a fanned stack is a busier silhouette.

Option P3

Display Box & Tray

The booster box gets its own object: a 3‑D shrink‑wrapped box that opens to a tray of 12 pack tops. You pull packs out of the tray one at a time instead of watching 72 cards fly by.

Why this one

  • Fixes the box, not the pack. Today a $110 box looks like a $10 pack with different text — the biggest purchase in the game has the smallest visual payoff.
  • Pacing. A 12‑pack tray gives a visible “how much is left”, and each pull is its own moment.
  • Guarantees get a home: the ≥3 ultra / ≥2 foil promise is printed on the box like a real case‑hit guarantee. The tray itself never says which pack is carrying it — every sealed pack looks identical until you tear it.
  • Pairs with either P1 or P2 — this is the box half of whichever pack you pick.

Cost

Three faces with rotation3DEffect, a gloss overlay and a tray grid. The tray also replaces the current “pack N of 12” counter.

3 · Opening it

Whichever pack you pick, the open is the best three seconds in the game. Two candidate gestures — try them both.

A · Tap to tear today, upgraded

One tap: the crimp strip rips off, the wrapper falls away and the card stack rises into place. Fast, accessible, one‑handed, and works identically for a box.

Keeps the current interaction contract — SealedPackView already tears on tap — so nothing to re‑learn, just a much better animation.

B · Swipe to rip tactile

Drag across the top crimp and the tear follows your finger, with a rising haptic rumble the further you pull. Release past ~60% and it rips; release early and it snaps back.

More satisfying, and the resistance sells the “physical” story. Needs a tap fallback for accessibility, and a first‑run hint so nobody gets stuck staring at a sealed pack.

Recommendation

P1

Foil Wrapper for the pack

Biggest “that’s a real pack” payoff per line of code, and it works at every size from a 58pt shop thumbnail to a full‑screen hero because the silhouette and the centred sigil do all the work. Add P3’s box and tray on top of it for booster boxes.

H1

Shelf List for home

Keeps everything that works about today’s screen — one scroll, all five sets, honest progress — and fixes what doesn’t: it shows the pack, promotes one action per set, demotes the box to a secondary, and pins cash so you always know what you can afford. H2 is the more exciting screen if you want the pack to be the whole home screen; H3 is the same idea with real charm but real art cost.

A

Tap to tear, with swipe as a stretch

Ship tap (it’s the current contract and it’s accessible), then add swipe‑to‑rip behind the same view once the wrapper exists.
